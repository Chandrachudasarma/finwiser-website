Real advice is a team. Few can afford one.

1 : 70,000
~1,000 SEBI-registered investment advisers for 7 crore+ salaried EPF members — and even they advise without EPF, income-tax or loan data

Real advice is a team — a cashflow analyst, a goal planner and a portfolio manager arguing until one answer survives. The wealthy hire that team. Everyone else gets dashboards.

Finwiser runs the team as one engine — a cashflow agent, a goals agent and a portfolio rules engine — live under a SEBI RIA licence on consented Account Aggregator data. Each missing rail blinds one of them, and a blinded specialist doesn't become cautious; it becomes confidently wrong.

It saves first, then decides

Lock in first

The cashflow agent's questions find the real surplus; you commit it — "Lock in ₹X for my plan" — before you spend.

Goals by priority

Must-haves funded first. Monthly category budgets nudge you as you near the limit.

Portfolio, checked

Your wealth mix, scored out of 100.

Most people spend first and invest whatever is left. Finwiser flips it: saving happens before spending, and expenses adapt to what's left. Live in the app today, shown on demo account data.

Emailed statements

What no rail carries yet — under a separate Gmail consent.

Six things happen, in production code: we read every account we receive; match AA and emailed copies once; categorise in eight tiers; spread annual dues over twelve months; turn the result into a surplus range, not a single number; and you lock in within it. The range narrows as the engine explains more of your transactions.

Then three specialists decide together, with real rules where they would conflict. Goals alone would fund the holiday first, or ask for more than you have — so must-haves come first, capped by the surplus. Cashflow missing an EMI would say your surplus is bigger — so when you tell us about an EMI paid from an account you haven't linked, confidence is held at 0.69, just below the 0.70 level at which the app acts on its own (0.40–0.70 it flags; below 0.40 it waits). Portfolio chasing returns would buy the top performer — so a fund must fit the goal, and EPF backs retirement only. A ranking layer — the Next Best Rupee — resolves it into one sentence: "Your next rupee goes to your emergency fund."

AI agents decide what to ask. Rules decide what to recommend.

Advice, not lending. Each new rail is a parser, not a product.

For the person

A retirement plan sized to what they actually have. The engine ranks the emergency fund and protection ahead of growth goals — better data raises a floor before it raises a return.

For inclusion

SEBI caps a fixed-fee adviser at ₹1.51 lakh a year per family. Finwiser offers free analysis and advice at ₹199 a month. EPF on the rail completes the picture for 7 crore+ EPF members the day EPFO joins; GST data already on the rail extends the same engine to the documented self-employed once our parser ships.

For the ecosystem

Periodic consent from people who aren't borrowing — a non-credit reason for EPFO to join the rail. The engine, consent flow and ranking are shared; cost grows with data fetches and compute, not headcount.

Prototype stage. Once the rail opens we will measure the share of users whose retirement plan changes on EPF inclusion, and consent completion for the new FI types.
